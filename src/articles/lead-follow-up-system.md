For nine years, I was the only salesperson at Upstack Studio.

Every enquiry came to me. Every request for a proposal came to me. I wrote them, I chased them, and I did all of it while running the rest of the company.

Some enquiries got a reply the same day. Some sat for a week. A few I never answered at all. Not because I didn't care. Because one person can only do so much, and I was the bottleneck for everything sales.

I knew. I just never made the time to look at it properly.

That's the part most owners recognise. You already suspect your follow-up is leaking. What you don't have is a quiet hour to prove it, and a way to decide what to do next.

So here's the hour. Four numbers, a way to read them, and a fix that doesn't start with buying software.

And yes, AI helps here. Just not the way it gets sold to you. AI is very good at reading a pile of enquiries and telling you what's in it. It's terrible at deciding what your business should do next. Count first. Then choose.

## First, check that this is actually your problem

Don't fix this because it sounds true. Most owners believe follow-up is their weak spot, and plenty of them are wrong. It costs you a week to find out.

Start with one list: every enquiry that arrived in the last 90 days. Email, web form, phone, WhatsApp, referral, the lot.

If that list doesn't exist anywhere, stop. That's your finding. You can't manage demand you can't see.

Once you have the list, pull four numbers.

**Number 1: how long until a human replied.** Not the auto-reply. A real person. Write down the middle value and the worst one.

**Number 2: how many got one attempt and nothing after.** One email, no reply, silence.

**Number 3: quotes sent, quotes chased, quotes closed.** That's three counts, not one. Most companies know the first and the third. Almost nobody has counted the middle one.

**Number 4: how many open enquiries have no owner and no next date.** Pick ten at random. For each one, can you name the person responsible and the thing that happens next? Count how many times you can't.

## How to read the four numbers

| What you counted | What a bad answer looks like | What it's telling you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to first human reply | Your worst case is many times your middle case | Speed depends on who happens to be free, not on a system |
| Enquiries with one attempt only | A big share of the list | The second attempt is nobody's job |
| Sent, chased, closed | Sent is far bigger than chased | You pay to produce quotes, then let them go quiet |
| No owner, no next date | You can't answer for most of the ten | There's no system here, just memory |

Notice what's missing. There's no industry benchmark in that table, because you don't need one. Your own numbers, compared against each other, tell you enough.

## A worked example

The figures below are invented to show the shape of it. They're not from a real company.

A company gets 120 enquiries in 90 days.

- Middle time to first reply: 6 hours. Worst: 9 days.
- 71 got one attempt and nothing after.
- 64 quotes sent. 18 chased. 11 closed.
- Of 10 open enquiries, 7 had no owner and no next date.

Read it back. The 6-hour middle says the team can be fast when it wants to be. The 9-day worst case says speed is luck. And 18 chased quotes out of 64 says the follow-up isn't slow, it's missing.

That company doesn't have a lead problem. It paid for 120 chances and used about 18 of them properly.

## The one-person test

Now the question that decided this for me.

Look at your list and ask who owns the follow-up. If the honest answer is one name, you've found your ceiling.

Do the sums on that person. A real follow-up takes about ten minutes. Read the history, write something worth reading, set the next date. At 120 enquiries and three touches each, that's 360 follow-ups a quarter. Sixty hours. Now add the proposals they write, the calls they take, and the job they already had.

That isn't a discipline problem. It's a capacity problem, and trying harder doesn't fix it. I was that one name for nine years.

## Where AI helps, and where it doesn't

AI is genuinely good at three jobs here.

- **Reading the pile.** Point it at 90 days of enquiry email and ask what's in there. What people asked for, which ones went quiet, which ones nobody answered. If your records are a mess, this is the fastest way to get numbers 1, 2 and 4.
- **Writing the first draft.** It reads the history and gives you something to edit in seconds. You still decide what goes out.
- **Watching for silence.** A machine never forgets that a quote went out eleven days ago and nobody replied.

It's bad at three others.

- **Holding the relationship.** The customer bought from a person.
- **Judging price and scope.** That's your risk, not the model's.
- **Deciding what the business does next.** Still your job.

So give the machine the counting, the watching and the first draft. Keep your people on the judgment.

One warning. If you buy an AI follow-up tool before you've counted, you'll automate a process nobody owns. Faster mess is still mess.

## Build the system in four parts

None of these start with buying anything.

1. **One list.** Every enquiry lands in one place, whichever way it arrived. Two lists means one of them is wrong.
2. **One owner per enquiry.** A name, not a team. Teams don't follow up. People do.
3. **A clock.** Every open enquiry has a next date. When that date passes, somebody sees it.
4. **A memory.** The history sits where the next person can read it, not in one person's head.

Get those four right on paper first. A whiteboard and a spreadsheet will do. Then ask which tool does them for you.

## What to buy, and in what order

Three questions before you pay for anything.

1. Does it hold the one list, or does it quietly become a second one?
2. Does it set the next date by itself, or does it wait for someone to remember?
3. Can the person doing the work use it on a phone, on a bad day, without training?

Then buy in this order: fix the process, use the shared inbox or CRM you already pay for, automate the reminders, and put AI on top of that. Most companies I met were shopping at the last step with nothing in place at the first three.

## Your next 30 days

- **Week 1.** Pull the four numbers.
- **Week 2.** Give every open enquiry an owner and a next date. Do it by hand. It's meant to be uncomfortable.
- **Week 3.** Watch what breaks. Whatever you forget twice is what you should automate.
- **Week 4.** Fix that one thing, and only that one thing.

Write your stop rule now, while you're calm. If the middle reply time and the one-attempt count haven't moved in 90 days, this is a capacity problem. You need another person, not another tool.
